### #455. Pull requests that wait to merge are tested together, once

- **Set:** `merge-group.yml` runs for the pull requests that wait to merge, one run at a time. Its `begin` job lists them, its `test` job applies them to `main` and runs the checks once, and its `merge` job merges them and waits for production. The timeouts are 10, 60 and 20 minutes. The `fits-main` job of `ci.yml` and `rerun-fits-main.yml` are gone, and the `merge` job of a `ci` run no longer waits for production, so its timeout is 5 minutes; the `ship` job of `approve-merge.yml` keeps its 20, for what follows a merge with GitHub's merge button (ticket #455; `decisions.md` [M455a](../decisions/455.md), [M16](../decisions/57.md), [M22](../decisions/57.md)).
- **Protects:** Actions minutes, and Claude usage.
- **Why:** each merge moved `main` under every other approved pull request, each of which was then tested alone again, and one left in conflict meanwhile took a fix round.
- **Numbers, before:** on 19 November 2026 six pull requests were approved between 15:47 and 15:48 UTC. In the 40 minutes that followed `rerun-fits-main.yml` ran 11 times and `fix.yml` 8 times. #422 was tested with `main` six times before it merged at 16:25, and #409 five times, with two fix rounds for a conflict. A test with `main` billed about 5 minutes: `rerun-fits-main.yml` 1, `fits-main` 3 (460 minutes in 153 jobs, [entry #388](388.md)) and the merge gate again 1. A fix round is about 20 minutes ([entry #388](388.md)).
- **Numbers, after:** worked out from those figures and not measured, because the workflow cannot run before it is installed.
  - A ticket that merges while nothing else waits: 2 billed minutes more than before, the `begin` job and the `merge` job, where the `ci` run's own merge job did both. No test runs: its `checks` job tested that merge ([entry 10](entry-10.md)).
  - Pull requests that wait together, or one under which `main` moved: one `test` job for the group, about 4 minutes (`checks` is 3.3, [entry #388](388.md)), where each was tested alone at about 5. The six of 19 November would have been two groups, the first approval and the five that followed it: about 12 billed minutes with `begin` and `merge`, against 11 runs of `rerun-fits-main.yml` at about 5 each.
  - A group whose checks fail: the checks run once more for each pull request in it, inside the same job, to find the one that fails (`decisions.md` [M455e](../decisions/455.md)).
  - `main` moving under a group, by a person's push or the workflow installer's: the run merges nothing and starts another, 3 jobs more.
  - A fault that is no pull request's own, such as a read of GitHub that fails or a test that leaves no result: the run merges nothing and starts another, 3 jobs more, twice at most before a person is asked (`decisions.md` [M455j](../decisions/455.md)).
  - A merged workflow change not yet installed: `begin` waits for it, up to 5 minutes of a job that already runs, where a test of the group would fail for every pull request in it (`decisions.md` [M455f](../decisions/455.md)).
  - Rejected, testing every group, a group of one included: about 4 billed minutes more for each ticket that merges alone.
- **What a ticket's cost leaves out:** a run of `merge-group.yml` serves every ticket of its group and runs on `main`, so `ticket-cost.sh` counts it for none; `minutes.sh` shows its minutes by workflow.
- **Event-driven:** the run is started by the event that changes what waits, a pull request passing the merge gate, and by `ship.sh` when it finds `main` moved. The one wait on a clock is `begin` reading `main` again every ten seconds while an install is on its way: the installer's push reaches no job that is already running.
- **Automated:** yes.
